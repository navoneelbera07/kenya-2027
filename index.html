Food guide

Tanzanian cooking runs from hearty mainland staples to the spice-soaked Swahili food of the coast — and Zanzibar, the original Spice Island, is a genuine highlight. Your no-beef rule is effortless here: goat, chicken and (on the coast) superb seafood carry everything.

🔥 On the mainland

Nyama choma — charcoal-grilled meat, the social ritual of Tanzania, best as goat or chicken. Mshikaki are marinated grilled skewers, great as street food.

Served with ugali (the maize staple), wali (rice) or chipsi (fries), plus kachumbari salad and sautéed mchicha greens.

Wash it down with a cold Kilimanjaro or Serengeti lager, or spiced chai.

🌊 On the coast (Zanzibar)

This is where it gets special. Zanzibar biryani and pilau, coconut fish curry (mchuzi wa samaki), grilled octopus, and wali wa nazi (coconut rice).

Street food at Forodhani night market in Stone Town: Zanzibar "pizza," urojo (a tangy mango-and-bhajia soup, Indian-influenced), sugarcane juice.

Spices everywhere — cloves, cardamom, nutmeg, vanilla — grown on the plantations you'll tour.

Dishes to seek out

All easily no-beef; the coast is a seafood-and-veg paradise.

What to actually buy

The things worth the (soft, 15 kg) bag.

Tanzanite
The violet-blue gem mined only near Kilimanjaro. Buy certified, from a reputable dealer, with paperwork — never on the street.
Tingatinga paintings
Tanzania's own vivid, whimsical art style — bright animals on canvas. Roll them for the flight home.
Makonde carvings
Intricate ebony sculpture from the Makonde people. Beautiful, but heavy — choose small.

Cloves, vanilla pods, cardamom, nutmeg — fresh, cheap, and the easiest gifts to bring back.

Printed cloth, each kanga carrying a Swahili proverb. Light, useful, endlessly giftable.

💉 Health — the good news

YELLOW FEVER: NOT REQUIRED

Because you fly in directly via the Gulf (not from Kenya), the yellow-fever certificate isn't a requirement. It's still sensible to get for safari travel, but it's no longer compulsory — a real plus for older travellers.

Malaria: the Serengeti and Zanzibar are risk areas — prophylaxis recommended; see a travel clinic 4–6 weeks out.

Insurance: must cover safari, ballooning (some policies exclude it), and evacuation.
